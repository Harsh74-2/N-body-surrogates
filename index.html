N-body ML Surrogates — Out-of-Distribution Solar-System Validation

A public benchmark of per-body, variable-N machine-learning surrogates for gravitational N-body dynamics, validated out of distribution on real Solar-System initial conditions from NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides.

Variable-body-count machine-learning surrogates for gravitational N-body dynamics, with out-of-distribution validation on real Solar-System initial conditions. Three architectures (MLP, LSTM, GNN) trained at body counts 10, 25, 50 and 100 on an immutably-frozen disc IMF dataset, then rolled out on 7 Solar-System presets from NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides.

Watch the simulations

Animated trajectory rollouts — leapfrog reference (dashed) vs. learned surrogate (solid) — for training counts N = 10/25/50/100, seven presets, and six model variants, plus interactive viewers with frame scrubbing, plane selection, and Catmull-Rom smoothing.

Error with vs. without autoregressive feedback (OOD mean)

The same checkpoints scored two ways, averaged over the six out-of-distribution presets. 'Single-step (no compounding)' rebuilds the input window from the true reference at every step, so prediction errors never feed back into the model -- it is scored at every point of the rollout horizon without feedback. 'Full rollout' lets predictions feed back (autoregressive), so errors compound. Diverged rollout cells (error above 100% of L) are excluded from the rollout mean, so the compounding factor is a lower bound. The gap between the two columns isolates the cost of error compounding: the same checkpoints hold roughly 5x lower error when their own predictions never feed back.
