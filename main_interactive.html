N-body ML Surrogates — Out-of-Distribution Benchmark

An open benchmark for machine-learning surrogates of gravitational N-body dynamics, validated against real Solar-System initial conditions.

Variable-body-count machine-learning surrogates for gravitational N-body dynamics, evaluated out-of-distribution on real Solar-System initial conditions. Three architectures (MLP, LSTM, GNN) are trained at N ∈ {10, 25, 50, 100} on a synthetic disc dataset drawn from a realistic initial-mass function, then rolled out on seven Solar-System presets sourced from NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides. This page is the visual companion to the benchmark; numerical audit tables, trajectory animations, static plots, and per-N dashboards are linked below.

Interactive trajectory viewers — rollout and single-step

Every (N, preset, architecture) combination has two kinds of animated, scrubbable trajectory viewer. Rollout viewers follow the autoregressive rollout: the surrogate feeds its own prediction back in, so small errors compound along the trajectory. Single-step viewers (suffixed singlestep) follow the single-step evaluation: each frame is one independent next-frame prediction from a warm-up window that is re-built from the reference every frame, so errors do not compound — the visual counterpart of the 1–3% in-distribution single-step error in the table above. In both, solid coloured markers = prediction, open grey markers + dashed grey lines = reference.

What this benchmark measures

  • Generalisation to real Solar-System initial conditions. Training is on synthetic disc IMF data; validation is on NASA JPL Horizons ephemerides. Three independent distribution shifts apply simultaneously: mass ratio (105× vs 10×), timestep (~50× finer), and geometry (thin-disk vs Plummer sphere).
  • Variable body count. One model per N, not one model handling all N. Tests whether a fixed-capacity network can absorb geometry/mass scaling without retraining.
  • Stability training. For each architecture there is a single-step-trained checkpoint and a stability-trained checkpoint (warm-started with a multi-step rollout loss). The stability variant lowers error variance but not always mean error.

How to read the results

  • Solid lines = surrogate prediction. Dashed lines = closed-form Kepler reference. Colours: blue = MLP, green = LSTM, red = GNN; the “_stable” suffix shares its base colour.
  • The disc IMF in-distribution baseline preset (lower-left of every grid) uses the same N, masses, timestep, and geometry as the training set. Its 1–3% error is the upper bound on surrogate quality.
  • The other six presets are all real Solar-System configurations. Error inflation is the cost of distribution shift, not surrogate failure.
